The class fundamentals modelREAL OUTPUT

Built from presidential approval, real income growth, and how many seats the president's party is defending. No polls, no markets, no candidate quality. Fitted on all 20 midterms from 1946 to 2022 and scored by leave-one-election-out error, because with n = 20 an in-sample R² means nothing.

R two-party House vote = 24.49 + 0.134 × approval (%) + 0.312 × real income growth (%) + 0.069 × seats held going in

Seats follow from the national margin

No simulation. The curve is deterministic given district baselines; all the uncertainty lives in one number, the national margin. Shaded band is the model's 80% interval carried through to seats.

The teachable result

Presidential approval on its own explains almost nothing about the national House vote — R² of 0.02 across twenty midterms. The referendum story only appears once you condition on exposure: adding approval to a model that already knows how many seats the president's party is defending improves out-of-sample error by 0.38 points. A party holding 290 seats is defending marginal ground it never should have won, and that, not the president's popularity, is what most of "the midterm penalty" turns out to be.

Fifteen races, sorted by how far apart the methods are. The races at the top are where forecasting is actually hard. Slate freezes 1 September and never changes.

Calibration seat · TX-11 — the safe seat is kept off the competitive axis rather than squeezed onto it. All four methods put the Republican between 22.0% and 23.1%, a spread of 1.1 points. If a student's model disagrees here, the model is broken, not brave. That is what the control is for.

Why this archive exists. ABC shut FiveThirtyEight down in March 2025. On 17 May 2026 Disney removed the archived site as well, and fivethirtyeight.com now redirects to ABC News. Nate Silver offered to buy the remaining intellectual property in order to restore the archive, and was refused.
