What the archive holds

Every forecaster we are permitted to collect, captured once a day and written to disk as raw bytes before anything is parsed. That ordering is the whole design: when a source changes its layout in October and a parser quietly starts returning nonsense, the stored bytes let us fix the parser and reprocess the entire history. If only parsed output were kept, that history would be gone.

What is not published, and why

Three separate restrictions, none of them the same thing.

Sources that permit collection but not republication

Several forecasters allow us to read and store their numbers but not to reprint them race by race during the cycle. Those appear only inside an average with at least three contributors, so no individual forecast can be backed out by subtraction. That floor is enforced in code, and the aggregator re-derives it from its own output and refuses to write if it is violated.

Licensed indices

District-level partisan lean is licensed. Because the national tide is public, a published district forecast would reveal the index exactly, so district numbers stay out of the published tier entirely.

What is published

Category averages, our own models in full, and a manifest of SHA-256 hashes for every capture, committed on the day it was taken. The hashes are a commitment without a disclosure: when the full archive is released after the election, anyone can verify the released bytes are bit-identical to what was collected on the dates claimed. Same logic as preregistration.
